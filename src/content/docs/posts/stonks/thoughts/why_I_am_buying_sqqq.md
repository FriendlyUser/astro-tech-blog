:::note[Looking back]
This is what I was thinking in April 2023. It didn't work out: the Nasdaq-100 rallied more than 50% over 2023, and SQQQ lost roughly 74% over the calendar year. The decay described below made that worse.
:::

With markets uncertain, I've been looking for a way to protect my portfolio from further downside. The one I've picked is the ProShares UltraPro Short QQQ ETF (SQQQ). This isn't advice; it's a record of my reasoning.

## What SQQQ does

SQQQ aims to return -3 times the Nasdaq-100's performance **for a single day**. If the index falls 1% today, SQQQ should rise about 3%; if the index rises 1%, SQQQ should fall about 3%. The fund resets its exposure every day, so over any period longer than a day its return is not -3x the index's return.

## Why I'm buying it

**It gains when the market falls.** Stocks and mutual funds I hold lose value in a sell-off. SQQQ rises, so it offsets some of those losses without me having to sell positions or short individual stocks.

**Leverage magnifies the move.** Because it's 3x, a drop in the index gives a bigger gain than an unleveraged short would, and I don't need to put much capital in to hedge a larger long position. The same leverage magnifies losses when the market goes up.

**The macro backdrop looks bad for tech.** The Fed has raised rates at the fastest pace in decades to fight inflation and has signalled that more hikes may come. That raises the odds of a recession, with higher borrowing costs and layoffs, and more losses in stocks.

I see two headwinds for the market over the next year:

- **Valuations.** Higher rates mean investors pay less for each dollar of future earnings (multiple compression). Long-duration growth stocks, which dominate the Nasdaq-100, are hit hardest.
- **Earnings.** If the economy slows, earnings estimates have to come down too.

Not everyone agrees. Some investors think higher rates will push stocks lower in the short term but set up a boom later. Others think they'll push the economy into recession and possibly a crash. I'm positioned for the second.

## The risks

**Daily reset causes decay.** In a choppy market SQQQ loses value even if the index ends up flat. Suppose the Nasdaq-100 falls 10% one day and rises 11.1% the next. The index is back where it started. SQQQ rises 30% (×1.30) and then falls 33.3% (×0.667), leaving it about 13% down. The longer you hold and the more volatile the market, the bigger this drag gets.

**It's volatile.** A 3% move in the index is a 9% move in SQQQ. A sharp rally can wipe out a large part of the position in days.

**Costs.** SQQQ's expense ratio is just under 1% a year, many times what a plain index fund charges, which adds to the drag over time.

**It's built for short holds.** ProShares says the fund is meant for short-term trading and should be monitored closely. I'm treating it as a hedge to be watched, not something to buy and forget.
