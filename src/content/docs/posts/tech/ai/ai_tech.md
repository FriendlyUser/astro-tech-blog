Software development is about to change because of AI and automation. A lot of the routine work engineers do today, from writing boilerplate to running regression tests, looks like it can be handed to AI tools. Done well, that means faster development and fewer bugs.

## Writing code

The most visible change is AI writing code. Tools such as [GitHub Copilot](https://github.com/features/copilot), originally built on OpenAI's Codex model, suggest code from a comment or the surrounding file, and chat models like ChatGPT can produce whole functions from a plain-English description. These assistants are best at the tedious, repetitive parts of programming, which frees engineers to spend more time on design and the hard problems.

## Requirements, testing and deployment

Coding is only one stage of the lifecycle, and AI can help in the others too:

- **Requirements.** Language models can read a specification and flag requirements that are ambiguous, vague or contradictory, so engineers can get them clarified before development starts.
- **Testing.** Tools can generate test cases aimed at increasing code coverage, produce synthetic test data for the application, and focus extra tests on the parts of the system that fail most often.
- **Deployment.** Rolling out updates is traditionally manual and easy to get wrong. Staged rollouts (release to a small percentage of users, fix problems, then widen) are already common practice; AI could help by predicting which changes are risky and watching the metrics during each stage.

## Humans are still needed

AI will reduce the need for some routine coding and testing work, but it won't replace engineers. It can't easily replicate the creativity, judgment and intuition good developers bring to a problem. I expect AI to be a tool that engineers use, and software development to become a collaboration between people and AI systems that lets teams ship faster with fewer defects.

## Beyond software

The same technology could help in other fields:

- **Healthcare.** Models trained on large datasets can spot patterns humans miss, for example in medical images used to diagnose cancer or eye disease, and could support personalised treatment plans and ease doctor shortages as populations age.
- **Transportation.** Road crashes kill about 1.16 million people a year, according to the [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/road-traffic-injuries). Self-driving vehicles could reduce that, and AI can also tune traffic signals and routes to cut congestion and make logistics cheaper.
- **Education.** AI tutors can adapt to a student's strengths and weaknesses, help with grading so teachers can focus on teaching, and bring good instruction to remote areas.

There are real risks, and the technology has to be developed carefully and fairly. But if it's applied responsibly, AI could be one of the most valuable technologies we've built, improving health, productivity and quality of life.
