AI labs need people to write, rate and correct model output, and a handful of platforms sell that work to remote contractors. Three names come up most: Outlier, Appen, and the newer Alignerr. Here's how they compare as of late 2025, what changed at Outlier, and why I think Alignerr is the best place to start.

## Outlier: the established player after a shake-up

Outlier is Scale AI's contractor platform and has long been a go-to for AI trainers, with a wide mix of tasks. That changed in June 2025, when Meta took a 49% stake in Scale AI (reported at about $14.3 billion) and Scale's CEO, Alexandr Wang, left to join Meta.

The fallout for contractors was quick. OpenAI and Google were reported to be winding down work with Scale, and in July 2025 Scale cut about 14% of its full-time staff (around 200 people) and ended work with about 500 contractors, saying it had grown its generative AI business too quickly.

**Pros**

- **Established platform**: mature systems for handing out tasks and paying people.
- **Variety of projects**: historically everything from data annotation to content evaluation.

**Cons**

- **Less work**: contractors have widely reported fewer available projects since the Meta deal.
- **Uncertain future**: with major customers pulling back, it's hard to count on Outlier long term.
- **More competition**: the remaining projects are spread across a larger pool of contractors, so steady work is harder to get.

## Appen: the veteran with a mixed reputation

Appen has been in the training-data business for a long time and is listed on the Australian Securities Exchange, which gives it a level of legitimacy many platforms lack. It advertises a crowd of over a million contributors and works with large tech companies.

**Pros**

- **Flexibility**: work from anywhere, at any time.
- **Accessibility**: many projects are beginner-friendly.
- **Legitimacy**: it's an established company that pays its contributors.

**Cons**

- **Low, inconsistent pay**: the most common complaint. Some tasks work out well below minimum wage.
- **Unpredictable workload**: like Outlier, not something to rely on as a main income.
- **Poor support**: many users describe unhelpful responses and little transparency.
- **Clunky platform**: the tooling is prone to glitches.

## Alignerr: the promising newcomer

Alignerr is Labelbox's network of AI trainers. It's younger than the other two and still has rough edges, but it's quickly becoming a favourite.

![Alignerr referral QR code](/imgs/2025/alignerr-referral-qr-code.png)

Alignerr is pitching itself on quality: more demanding work, better paid. It doesn't have Outlier's or Appen's track record, but that's the trade-off it's making.

**Pros**

- **Higher pay**: many contributors report noticeably better rates than on other platforms, and some projects pay well enough to be a real income.
- **More engaging projects**: the work leans towards complex tasks, which suits people with domain knowledge and an eye for detail.
- **Growing**: the number and range of projects keeps increasing.
- **Responsive**: the team seems to act on contributor feedback.

**Cons**

- **Inconsistent availability**: work can still come and go, though that should improve as the platform grows.
- **Communication gaps**: a common problem for a fast-growing platform.
- **Growing pains**: some parts of the system are still being worked out.

## Verdict: why Alignerr comes out on top

Outlier and Appen dominated AI training work for years, but Outlier's post-Meta slowdown and Appen's long-running pay and support problems leave an opening. Alignerr's focus on higher-quality work, better pay and steady improvement makes it the most promising option for AI trainers right now, early days and all.

If you're getting into AI training, or looking for something better than the established platforms, Alignerr is the clear choice.
